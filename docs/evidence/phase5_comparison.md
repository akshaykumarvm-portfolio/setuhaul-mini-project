# Phase 5: CP-SAT vs dispatch-rule baseline (Jaipur DC, 4 Aug 2026 snapshot)

Generated by `python -m booking.scheduling`. Both methods read the same structured state, obey the same rules
(checked by one validator) and are scored on the same objective (priority-weighted lateness vs due time, then
waiting, then changed promises; approvals and hand-offs heavily penalised). CP-SAT starts from the baseline plan as a
hint, so within its 5 s limit it can only match or beat it. Trucks with contradictory data (driver on two trucks, A8)
count at the lowest weight: they never displace confirmed work. Proposals only: nothing here is booked.

## Verdict

* Every plan from both methods is valid at every time tested; CP-SAT is **never worse** and is **strictly better on
  the objective at 6 of 9 snapshot times**.
* At **10:00, the brief's competing-trucks moment, compare the two columns below**: where CP-SAT only ties, the
  console recommends the simpler dispatch rule and says so (Decision 8: keep the baseline unless CP-SAT beats it).
* Both are far better than holding the current promises, many of which are already impossible (trucks booked before
  they can arrive, a 21 t load on a 20 t dock, slots inside a breakdown).
* Recommendation: dispatch rule as the default, CP-SAT as an advisor on re-plan triggers; adopt its plan only when
  the objective improves, and every change still goes through hold -> request -> coordinator confirm.
